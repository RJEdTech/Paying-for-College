How the numbers are worked out

College costs and loans. Each college starts from its published cost for 2025–26 (College Scorecard). For private colleges and Colorado public colleges, the price after grants is what families in your income range paid on average; out-of-state publics have no such average for Colorado families, so their full price is shown (type in any scholarship by changing the number). The price rises 3% a year. Loans follow the same model as Who Borrows?: 2026–27 federal rates and fees, the new Parent PLUS limits, interest during school, and the Tiered Standard plan (10 to 25 years by balance).

Living costs. MIT Living Wage Calculator (updated February 15, 2026) for the metro area and household: food, child care, health care (insurance premiums and care), housing (HUD fair market rent with basic utilities), transportation (a car), civic, internet and phone, and other necessities. These are basic needs only: no eating out, vacations or savings. Sharing a 2-bedroom is half of MIT’s 2-bedroom rent. Buying a home replaces rent with a 30-year mortgage, taxes and insurance, and utilities.

Pay needed. The yearly pay at which take-home pay covers the living costs, the student’s loan payment and the savings you chose, after 2026 federal income tax (single, or married filing jointly with the $2,200 child tax credit per child), Social Security and Medicare, and the metro’s state income tax (Colorado 4.4% plus 0.44% FAMLI, Arizona 2.5%, Illinois 4.95%, Utah 4.45%, none in Texas, Tennessee and Washington). For couples, pay is split evenly between two workers. Parent PLUS payments are the parents’, so they are not in the student’s budget.

What jobs pay. Early-career pay by major: Georgetown University Center on Education and the Workforce, The Major Payoff (2025), full-time workers ages 22 to 26 with only a bachelor’s degree, national, in 2024 dollars (a quarter earn less than the low number and a quarter more than the high one). Local pay by kind of work: typical salaries for all experience levels in the metro (MIT, from the Bureau of Labor Statistics). Minimum wage: the state’s rate in MIT’s data, full time for a year (2,080 hours); Denver’s city minimum is higher, $19.29.

Rules of thumb. Total student debt below the first year’s pay, and a loan payment under 10% of pay (Mark Kantrowitz and other aid experts, via CNBC); parents’ total college debt for all children below one year’s income, and less near retirement; housing at or under 30% of income (HUD). Guidelines, not rules.

This is a planning estimate, not financial advice. Real pay, rents, rates and taxes will differ.
