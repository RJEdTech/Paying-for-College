What each offer really costs

Before you decide: ask each financial aid office

  1. Is this offer for 2027–28, or does it use last year’s costs?
  2. Which grants renew each year, and what GPA or credits keep them?
  3. If my student wins an outside scholarship, do you lower loans first or your own grants?
  4. Are books, travel and personal costs included in the cost you listed?
  5. Can we appeal this offer if our finances changed, or if another college offered more?
  6. When is the first bill due, and is there a monthly payment plan?
How the numbers are worked out
  • Net price = cost of attendance − free money (grants and scholarships). This is the price your family covers with savings, income or loans.
  • Still to pay = net price − the loans and work-study in the offer. Accepting the loans moves that part of the cost into the future; it doesn’t remove it.
  • First bill counts only tuition and housing, the parts a college bills. Books, travel and personal costs are real but are paid as you go.
  • Four years: costs rise by the rate you choose. College awards stay the same each year unless you mark them first-year only. Federal and state grants are assumed to repeat (they depend on each year’s FAFSA). Outside scholarships count for year 1 only unless you mark that they renew.
  • These are planning estimates, not a bill. The college’s financial aid office has the final numbers.
