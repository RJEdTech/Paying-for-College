How the numbers are worked out
  • Each year: net price (rising by your %) − what your family and student pay from income − savings put down = the amount to finance. Savings go to the first years’ bills unless you choose to spread them evenly.
  • Plan A uses the student’s federal loan first, up to the yearly limit, then Parent PLUS for the rest. Plan B uses Parent PLUS for all of it.
  • Fees come out of each loan, so a family borrows a little more than it needs. For example, $20,000 of Parent PLUS delivers $19,154.
  • Each year’s loan is paid out in two halves, in August and January. No payments are made during school; interest builds and is added to the balance when payments start, 6 months after graduation. Parents can ask for this same deferment, or start paying sooner and pay less interest.
  • The repayment term comes from the balance when payments start: 10, 15, 20 or 25 years. Payments are the same every month.
  • If part of the student loan is subsidized (based on need), the government pays that part’s interest during school, so the student’s real cost is a little lower than shown.
  • A private loan, if you add one, is shown over 10 years with no fee. Real terms vary by lender.
  • These are planning estimates, not a loan offer.
