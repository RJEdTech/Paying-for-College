What changed for 2026–27

Several rules changed this year, and most families don’t hear about them until an award letter arrives.

ChangeWhat it means for your family
Parent PLUS limitsParents who first borrow on or after July 1, 2026 can borrow at most $20,000 per student per year and $65,000 per student in total. Borrowing the full $20,000 each year hits the total before the fourth year. Northwestern
Parent PLUS cost and repayment9.07% fixed for 2026–27, plus a 4.228% fee taken out of each loan. New Parent PLUS loans can only be repaid on the Tiered Standard plan: no income-based plan and no Public Service Loan Forgiveness. Union College, Credible (NASFAA table)
Student loansA dependent student can borrow $5,500 in the first year, $6,500 in the second and $7,500 in each later year ($31,000 in total), at 6.52% for 2026–27 with a 1.057% fee. The loan is in the student’s name. Marquette
Repayment term grows with the balanceNew federal loans are repaid over 10 years if the balance is under $25,000, 15 years up to $50,000, 20 years up to $100,000, and 25 years above that. ELFI
Pell Grant limitsNo Pell Grant with a Student Aid Index of $14,790 or more, or when other grants already cover the full cost. UC Santa Barbara
Colorado aid at private collegesFor 2026–27, Colorado paused its need-based aid and work-study at the state’s private colleges, such as Regis University and the University of Denver: $14.1 million for about 2,000 students. The College Opportunity Fund stipend continues. Ask these colleges how they are filling the gap. Chalkbeat
FAFSA for 2027–28Opened September 23, 2026. Many state and college deadlines start in January. PCS Classical
CSS Profile for 2027–28Opened October 1, 2026. It costs $25 for the first college and $16 for each one after; some families qualify for a waiver. 10,000 Degrees
Colorado’s biggest scholarships close earlyDaniels: September 1 to October 15 of senior year. Boettcher: September 1 to November 1 of senior year ($20,000 a year for four years at a Colorado college). Juniors should plan in the spring. Daniels Fund, Boettcher Foundation, UCCS
Western discount (WUE)Participating public colleges in other Western states charge no more than 150% of their in-state tuition. WICHE flyer
Colorado 529 deductionFor 2026, deposits to a CollegeInvest 529 can be deducted from Colorado taxable income up to $26,200 per student for a single filer, or $39,200 on a joint return. CollegeInvest
Outside scholarships can shrink college aidAbout half of colleges lower their own aid when a student brings in an outside scholarship. Ask each college for its policy before you accept. Georgetown

Questions to ask every financial aid office

  1. Is this offer for 2027–28, or does it use last year’s costs?
  2. Which grants renew each year, and what GPA or credits keep them?
  3. If my student wins an outside scholarship, do you lower loans first or your own grants?
  4. Are books, travel and personal costs included in the cost you listed?
  5. Can we appeal this offer if our finances changed, or if another college offered more?
  6. When is the first bill due, and is there a monthly payment plan?

Talk it through together

Money is one part of a bigger question: where will your student grow? A few questions, asked before offers arrive in the spring, can turn a stressful decision into a shared one.

  • What can our family pay each year without new debt? Does our student know that number?
  • How much, if anything, is our student willing to borrow, and what monthly payment would that mean after graduation?
  • What matters most to our student in a college, and what is it worth to us?
  • If two colleges both fit, how will we decide between them?
  • Who else depends on these savings: brothers and sisters, or our own retirement?
